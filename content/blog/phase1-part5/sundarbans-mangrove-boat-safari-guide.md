The **Sundarbans**, where the Ganga, Brahmaputra and Meghna rivers meet the Bay of Bengal, is the **largest mangrove forest in the world**. The Indian side, in West Bengal, is a **UNESCO World Heritage Site** and a tiger reserve, home to **Royal Bengal tigers** that swim between islands, saltwater crocodiles, and a maze of tidal creeks. Here, the safari is by **boat**, gliding through narrow channels lined with mangroves.

## How to reach the Sundarbans

- **From Kolkata:** most trips start with a drive of about **3 hours** (around 100 km) to **Godkhali** or **Sonakhali** jetty, from where boats head into the delta.
- **By train:** local trains run from Kolkata's Sealdah station to **Canning**, followed by a road transfer to the jetty.
- **By air:** **Kolkata**'s Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport is the nearest.

## How a Sundarbans trip works

Most visitors take a **1 to 3-day package** from Kolkata that includes transfers, a boat, meals, a guide and permits. You stay in lodges or eco-resorts on islands such as **Gosaba, Pakhirala** and **Bali**, or on the boat itself.

- **Permits:** entry to the tiger reserve requires permits from the forest department, usually arranged by your operator. Carry photo ID.
- **Boat safaris:** you cruise through the rivers and creeks, stopping at watchtowers inside the reserve. Walking in the forest is not allowed.

## Watchtowers and stops

- **Sajnekhali:** the main watchtower and the **Mangrove Interpretation Centre**, with a small museum, a crocodile pond and a turtle hatchery.
- **Sudhanyakhali:** a watchtower overlooking a freshwater pond, known for tiger sightings.
- **Dobanki:** a **canopy walk** on a raised, fenced walkway through the mangroves.
- **Netidhopani:** a remote watchtower with the ruins of an ancient temple.
- **Burirdabri** and **Jhingakhali:** further watchtowers, reached on longer trips.

Some watchtowers need extra permits or longer boat journeys.

## Wildlife

- **Royal Bengal tiger:** the Sundarbans tigers are adapted to swim and live in the tidal forest. They are elusive, and **sightings are rare**, so enjoy the forest and the other wildlife.
- **Saltwater crocodiles**, often basking on mudbanks.
- **Spotted deer**, **wild boar** and **rhesus macaques**.
- **Water monitor lizards** and **Olive Ridley** and other turtles.
- **Birds:** kingfishers of many kinds, herons, egrets, and in winter, migratory birds.
- **Gangetic and Irrawaddy dolphins** in the larger rivers.

## Life in the delta

The islands of the Sundarbans are home to fishing and farming communities. Many trips include village walks and cultural performances, such as the **Bonbibi** folk play about the forest goddess who protects people from tigers. Honey collection and crab fishing are traditional livelihoods, and the forest's dangers are part of daily life.

## Best time to visit

- **October to March:** the best season, with cool weather, calm waters and migratory birds.
- **April to June:** hot and humid, but good for sightings as animals come to fresh water.
- **Monsoon (July to September):** heavy rain and rough waters; some trips are suspended, and the tiger reserve may have restrictions.

## Choosing an operator

- Book with a **licensed operator** registered with West Bengal tourism or the forest department.
- Check that the **boat has life jackets**, a toilet and a covered deck.
- Ask what's included: permits, meals, guides and the watchtowers covered.
- Prefer operators that follow **responsible practices**, with no loud music, no littering and respect for wildlife.

## A 2-day plan

1. **Day 1:** drive from Kolkata to Godkhali, boat to your lodge; afternoon cruise to Sajnekhali; village evening with a Bonbibi performance.
2. **Day 2:** early boat safari to Sudhanyakhali and Dobanki, then return to Kolkata by evening.

A 3-day trip lets you reach remote watchtowers such as Netidhopani.

## Safety tips

- **Stay inside the boat and within fenced watchtower areas.**
- **Never step onto the mudbanks** or into the water; tigers and crocodiles are real dangers.
- Carry mosquito repellent, sunscreen and a hat.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you see tigers in the Sundarbans?

Sightings are rare because tigers are elusive in the dense mangroves, but they do happen. Most visitors see crocodiles, deer, birds and monkeys.

### How many days are needed for the Sundarbans?

Two days is enough for a first visit; three days lets you reach more remote watchtowers.

### Do I need a permit for the Sundarbans?

Yes. Entry to the tiger reserve needs a forest department permit, usually arranged by your operator.
