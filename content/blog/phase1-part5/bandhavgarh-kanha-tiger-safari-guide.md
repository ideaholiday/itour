Madhya Pradesh has more tigers than any other state in India, and two of its reserves are among the best places in the world to see them. **Bandhavgarh** is famous for its high density of tigers and dramatic rocky hills, while **Kanha**, with its vast sal forests and meadows, is often said to have inspired the landscapes of The Jungle Book, and is home to the rare hard-ground barasingha. Both are ideal for a first tiger safari.

## Bandhavgarh National Park

### How to reach

- **By train:** **Umaria** station is about 35 km away; **Katni** has more trains.
- **By air:** **Jabalpur** Airport is about 200 km away, around 4 hours by road.
- Most lodges are in the village of **Tala**, next to the main gate.

### Safari zones

- **Tala:** the most famous core zone, with meadows, the **Bandhavgarh Fort** on its hill, and many tiger sightings.
- **Magadhi:** open grasslands and forest, excellent for tigers.
- **Khitauli:** a quieter zone with good birding and sightings.
- Several **buffer zones** are open for part of the year when core zones are closed.

**Bandhavgarh Fort** stands on a hilltop inside the park, and its ancient statues, including a giant reclining Vishnu, can be seen on some routes.

## Kanha National Park

### How to reach

- **By air:** **Jabalpur** is about 160 to 170 km away, around 4 hours by road. **Raipur** and **Nagpur** are other options.
- **By train:** **Jabalpur** and **Gondia** are the nearest major stations.
- Lodges are around the **Mukki** and **Khatia (Kisli)** gates.

### Safari zones

- **Kanha:** the central zone, with the famous meadows of Kanha and Shravantal.
- **Kisli:** forest and meadows near the Khatia gate.
- **Mukki:** in the south-east, with dense sal forest.
- **Sarhi:** a quieter zone in the south.
- Buffer zones such as **Khatia** and **Phen** add more options.

**Kanha Museum** and the **Bamni Dadar** (Sunset Point) are worth visiting.

## Which should you choose?

- **Bandhavgarh:** best for tiger sightings in a short visit, thanks to its high density of tigers.
- **Kanha:** a larger, more varied park with beautiful meadows, more wildlife variety, and the barasingha. Great for a slower, more immersive trip.
- **Both:** they are about 250 km apart, and many travellers combine them in 5 to 7 days. **Pench** National Park is another option nearby.

## How to book a safari

- Safaris are booked on the **official Madhya Pradesh Forest Department online booking portal**. Permits are limited per zone and per slot, and open well in advance; popular zones sell out quickly.
- You need **ID details for every guest**, and must carry the same IDs at the gate.
- **Jeep safaris** (Gypsy) take up to six guests with a registered driver and guide. Some parks also offer **canter** safaris.
- There are **morning and afternoon slots**; mornings are usually best.

Lodges and licensed operators can book for you, but make sure you receive the official permit.

## When are the parks open?

- The **core zones** of Madhya Pradesh's tiger reserves are usually open from **October 1 to June 30**, and **closed during the monsoon** (July to September).
- Some **buffer zones** may stay open in the monsoon.

**Best time:**

- **October to February:** cool, green and pleasant; winter mornings are very cold in open jeeps.
- **March to June:** hot, but the best time for tiger sightings, as animals come to water holes.

## Tips for a good safari

- **Book multiple safaris**, ideally across different zones.
- **Wear muted colours** and dress in layers for winter mornings.
- **Carry binoculars**, a hat, sunscreen and water.
- **Keep quiet**, stay in the jeep, and follow the guide's instructions.
- **Don't pressure drivers** to speed or crowd a tiger; respect the rules.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is better for tiger sightings, Bandhavgarh or Kanha?

Bandhavgarh usually gives better chances in a short visit. Kanha is larger and more varied, with excellent sightings too.

### When are Bandhavgarh and Kanha closed?

The core zones are usually closed from July 1 to September 30 for the monsoon.

### How do I book a safari?

On the official Madhya Pradesh Forest Department online portal, with ID details for every guest.
