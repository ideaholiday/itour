**Rameswaram**, on Pamban Island off the coast of Tamil Nadu, is one of the most sacred places in India. It is one of the Char Dham, home to one of the twelve **Jyotirlingas**, and closely linked to the Ramayana: it was from here that Lord Rama is said to have built the bridge to Lanka. At the island's tip lies **Dhanushkodi**, a ghost town destroyed by a cyclone in 1964, where the Bay of Bengal meets the Indian Ocean.

## How to reach Rameswaram

- **By train:** Rameswaram station has direct trains from Chennai, Madurai and other cities, crossing the sea on the **Pamban rail bridge**.
- **By road:** the **Annai Indira Gandhi Road Bridge** links the island with the mainland. Madurai is about 170 km away, around 3.5 hours by road.
- **By air:** **Madurai** is the nearest airport.

## The Pamban bridges

The old **Pamban rail bridge**, opened in 1914, was India's first sea bridge. It has been replaced by the **new Pamban bridge**, India's first vertical-lift sea bridge, which lifts to let ships pass. Stop on the road bridge alongside to see the rail bridge and the turquoise sea.

## Ramanathaswamy Temple

The **Ramanathaswamy Temple** is dedicated to Shiva as Ramanathaswamy. According to tradition, Rama worshipped Shiva here after his return from Lanka.

- **The corridors:** the temple's pillared outer corridors are among the **longest temple corridors in India**, with rows of carved pillars stretching into the distance.
- **The 22 theerthams:** inside the temple are **22 wells** (theerthams), each believed to have different sacred qualities. Pilgrims bathe with water drawn from each one before darshan. Attendants pour the water over you for a fee; wear clothes you don't mind getting wet, and carry a change.
- **Agni Theertham:** the sea in front of the temple, where pilgrims bathe first.

**Rules:** phones and cameras are usually not allowed inside. Dress traditionally; men may be asked to wear a dhoti. Non-Hindus may not be allowed into the inner sanctum.

## Other sights in Rameswaram

- **Panchmukhi Hanuman Temple:** with floating stones said to be from the Ram Setu.
- **Gandhamadhana Parvatham:** a small hill with the footprints of Rama and views over the island.
- **Kothandaramaswamy Temple:** on the road to Dhanushkodi, the only structure in the area to survive the 1964 cyclone.
- **Dr APJ Abdul Kalam National Memorial** at Peikarumbu, and the **house museum** of the former President, who was born in Rameswaram.

## Dhanushkodi

About 20 km from Rameswaram, **Dhanushkodi** was a busy town until the **1964 cyclone** destroyed it. Today you can see the ruins of the church, railway station and post office on a narrow strip of sand between two seas.

- **Arichal Munai:** the very tip of the island, where the road ends and the Bay of Bengal meets the Indian Ocean. It is the closest point in India to Sri Lanka.
- **The road:** a new road runs to Arichal Munai; visits are usually allowed only during daylight hours.
- **Swimming:** the sea here can be rough and swimming is not safe. Stay on the sand.

Go early in the morning or late afternoon, and carry water and sun protection.

## Best time to visit

**October to April** is the most comfortable time. **Mahashivratri** and the **Aadi Amavasai** and **Thai Amavasai** new moon days, when many perform rites for their ancestors, draw huge crowds. Summers are hot, and the northeast monsoon (October to December) brings rain and occasionally cyclones.

## A 2-day plan

1. **Day 1:** arrive via the Pamban bridge; bathe at Agni Theertham and in the 22 theerthams, and darshan at Ramanathaswamy Temple.
2. **Day 2:** Dhanushkodi and Arichal Munai at sunrise, Kothandaramaswamy Temple, Kalam memorial, Panchmukhi Hanuman and Gandhamadhana Parvatham.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does the 22 theertham bath take?

About an hour, longer on busy days.

### Can I visit Dhanushkodi at night?

Usually not. Visits are generally allowed only in daylight hours.

### How many days do I need in Rameswaram?

One to two days covers the temple, Dhanushkodi and the main sights.
