Delhi has been the capital of empires for over a thousand years, and it shows. In three days you can walk through a Mughal bazaar, see three UNESCO World Heritage Sites, eat some of the best street food in India and still find time for leafy gardens and modern cafés. The Delhi Metro makes it easy to get around, and the city is a natural starting point for Agra, Jaipur and the hills.

## Getting around Delhi

- **Delhi Metro:** fast, clean and cheap. Buy a smart card or use QR tickets. The Airport Express line connects the airport to central Delhi.
- **Cabs and autos:** app-based cabs and autos are widely available. For street autos, agree the fare first or ask for the meter.
- **Walking:** Old Delhi is best on foot or by cycle-rickshaw.

Tip: many monuments are closed on one weekday. The Red Fort is closed on Mondays, and Akshardham and the Lotus Temple are also closed on Mondays, so plan them for other days.

## Day 1: Old Delhi

### Red Fort

The massive red sandstone fort built by Shah Jahan, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. See the Diwan-i-Aam, Diwan-i-Khas and the museums inside. The evening sound and light show tells the story of the fort. Buy tickets online to save queue time.

### Jama Masjid

One of India's largest mosques, also built by Shah Jahan. Climb the southern minaret, if open, for a view over Old Delhi. Dress modestly; robes are available at the gate. Avoid prayer times, especially Friday afternoon.

### Chandni Chowk

The old Mughal bazaar is chaotic and wonderful. Walk through Kinari Bazaar for wedding trims, Dariba Kalan for silver, and Khari Baoli, Asia's largest spice market. See Gurudwara Sis Ganj Sahib and the Jain Lal Mandir along the way.

**Eat:** parathas at Paranthe Wali Gali, jalebis, chaat, and kebabs and nihari near Jama Masjid.

### Evening: Raj Ghat or Gurudwara Bangla Sahib

End the day at the memorial to Mahatma Gandhi at Raj Ghat, or at Gurudwara Bangla Sahib near Connaught Place, where the free langar feeds thousands every day and anyone can volunteer.

## Day 2: Mughal tombs and New Delhi

### Humayun's Tomb

The garden tomb that inspired the Taj Mahal, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Go in the morning when the light is soft. Next door, the **Sunder Nursery** is a beautifully restored heritage park.

### Hazrat Nizamuddin Dargah

The shrine of the Sufi saint Nizamuddin Auliya. On Thursday evenings, qawwali singers perform in the courtyard. Cover your head and dress modestly.

### Lodhi Garden

Tombs of the Sayyid and Lodi dynasties set in a large park where Delhiites jog and picnic. Walk to the nearby Lodhi Art District for murals.

### India Gate and Kartavya Path

The war memorial and the redeveloped ceremonial boulevard look best in the evening when lit up. The National War Memorial is right next door.

**Eat:** Khan Market or Connaught Place for dinner.

## Day 3: Qutub Minar, Akshardham and markets

### Qutub Minar

The 73-metre victory tower begun in the late 12th century, surrounded by ruins, mosques and the famous iron pillar that has not rusted for centuries. A UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Mehrauli Archaeological Park or Hauz Khas

Explore step-wells and tombs in Mehrauli, or walk around the medieval reservoir and ruins of Hauz Khas Village, which also has cafés and boutiques.

### Lotus Temple

The Bahá'í House of Worship, shaped like a lotus, is open to all faiths for silent prayer.

### Swaminarayan Akshardham

A vast carved stone temple complex with exhibitions and an evening water show. Phones, cameras and bags are not allowed inside, so keep them in the cloakroom or leave them in your hotel. Allow at least three to four hours.

**Shop:** Dilli Haat for crafts from every state, Sarojini Nagar for bargains, or Janpath for souvenirs.

## What to eat in Delhi

- **Chole bhature** and **chole kulche**.
- **Butter chicken** and **dal makhani**, which many say began in Delhi.
- **Chaat:** aloo tikki, papdi chaat, golgappe and dahi bhalla.
- **Mughlai food** near Jama Masjid.
- **Momos:** Delhi's favourite snack.
- **Kulfi** and **daulat ki chaat** in winter.

## Best time to visit Delhi

October to March is the best season, though air quality can be poor in November and December, so check the AQI and carry a mask. Winter mornings can be foggy. Summers (April to June) are very hot, and the monsoon comes in July and August.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 3 days enough for Delhi?

Yes, for the main sights. Add a day for a day trip or for more museums, such as the National Museum and the Pradhanmantri Sangrahalaya.

### Which monuments in Delhi are closed on Monday?

The Red Fort, Akshardham and the Lotus Temple are closed on Mondays. Check each site before you go, as rules can change.

### How do I avoid touts in Delhi?

Book tickets online, use app-based cabs, and ignore anyone at stations who says your hotel is closed or offers "official" tourist offices.
