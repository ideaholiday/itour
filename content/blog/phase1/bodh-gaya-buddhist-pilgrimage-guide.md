Bodh Gaya in Bihar is the most sacred place in Buddhism: it was here, under a pipal tree, that Prince Siddhartha attained enlightenment and became the Buddha. Today the small town is home to the UNESCO-listed Mahabodhi Temple and to monasteries built by Buddhist communities from across Asia. It is peaceful, affordable and easy to combine with Rajgir, Nalanda and Varanasi.

## How to reach Bodh Gaya

- **By air:** Gaya Airport is about 12 km from the temple, with domestic flights and seasonal international flights from Buddhist countries. Patna airport has more connections and is about 3 to 4 hours away by road.
- **By train:** Gaya Junction, about 15 km away, is on the main Delhi–Kolkata line.
- **By road:** Patna is about 115 km away and Varanasi about 250 km.

## The Mahabodhi Temple

The Mahabodhi Temple complex was listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2002. The tall pyramidal tower rises about 55 metres above the site where the Buddha meditated, and the complex is one of the earliest Buddhist temples built entirely in brick in India.

Inside the complex:

- **The Bodhi Tree:** a descendant of the original tree under which the Buddha sat. Pilgrims meditate, chant and tie prayer flags around it.
- **The Vajrasana (Diamond Throne):** a stone platform marking the exact spot of enlightenment.
- **Animeshlochana Chaitya and the Cloister Walk:** marking where the Buddha spent the weeks after enlightenment.
- **The meditation park and the Lotus Pond:** quieter corners for sitting.

**Visiting tips:** go at dawn or late evening, when chanting monks from many traditions fill the complex. Remove your shoes at the entrance. Photography rules and fees can change, so check at the gate; phones may need to be deposited.

## The international monasteries

One of the joys of Bodh Gaya is walking between monasteries built in the architectural styles of different countries:

- **Royal Thai Monastery:** golden roofs in the classic Thai style.
- **Japanese Indosan Nipponji Temple:** a calm wooden temple.
- **Tibetan Monasteries:** colourful prayer halls and prayer wheels.
- **Royal Bhutan Monastery:** intricate clay carvings inside.
- Monasteries from **Vietnam, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Nepal and China** as well.

Most are open to visitors in the morning and afternoon. Enter quietly and follow the dress and shoe rules at each one.

## The Great Buddha Statue

A short walk from the main temple, the Great Buddha Statue is an 80-foot seated Buddha of sandstone and red granite, unveiled by the Dalai Lama in 1989. It is surrounded by statues of the Buddha's ten main disciples.

## Sujata Village and the Dungeshwari Caves

Across the Niranjana (Falgu) river lies the village of **Sujata**, where a young woman is said to have offered the Buddha rice pudding before his enlightenment; the Sujata Stupa marks the spot. The **Dungeshwari (Mahakala) Caves**, about 12 km away, are where the Buddha practised severe austerities before he chose the Middle Way.

## Day trip: Rajgir and Nalanda

About 80 to 90 km from Bodh Gaya, this is the classic day trip:

- **Nalanda Mahavihara:** the ruins of one of the world's oldest residential universities, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2016. The Nalanda Archaeological Museum is well worth an hour.
- **Rajgir:** Vulture's Peak (Gridhakuta), where the Buddha gave many sermons, and the Vishwa Shanti Stupa, reached by an aerial ropeway. Rajgir also has hot springs and a glass bridge in its nature safari park.

Leave early; the roads are good but the sights are spread out.

## Best time to visit

October to March is the best season: the weather is pleasant and this is when most pilgrims and monks arrive. **Buddha Purnima** (April or May) is the most important festival. The Dalai Lama has held Kalachakra teachings here, and large prayer gatherings take place in winter, so book stays early for December and January. Summers are very hot.

## Where to stay and eat

Bodh Gaya has everything from simple guesthouses and monastery guest rooms to comfortable hotels close to the temple. Food is mostly vegetarian, with Tibetan cafés serving thukpa and momos, Thai and Japanese dishes, and Bihari classics like litti chokha.

## Combine Bodh Gaya with Varanasi and Sarnath

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Bodh Gaya?

One full day covers the Mahabodhi Temple, the monasteries and the Great Buddha Statue. Add a day for Rajgir and Nalanda.

### Do I have to be Buddhist to visit the Mahabodhi Temple?

No. Visitors of all faiths are welcome. Dress modestly, keep quiet and respect people meditating.

### Is Bodh Gaya safe for travellers?

Yes. It is a small, calm pilgrim town. Use the same care you would in any busy place and avoid walking alone late at night in unlit areas.
