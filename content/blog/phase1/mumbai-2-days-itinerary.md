Mumbai is India's most energetic city: the home of Bollywood, the country's financial capital, and a place of grand Victorian buildings, sea-facing promenades and legendary street food. Two days is enough to see its highlights, from the Gateway of India and the Elephanta Caves to sunset on Marine Drive and a vada pav at a crowded railway stall.

## Getting around Mumbai

- **Local trains:** the fastest way to cover long distances. Avoid peak hours (roughly 8 to 11 am and 5 to 9 pm) and use the ladies' compartments if you are a woman travelling alone. First class is worth it for visitors.
- **Metro:** Mumbai's metro network is growing fast and is air-conditioned and easy to use.
- **Taxis and autos:** black-and-yellow taxis run on the meter in South Mumbai; autos are allowed only in the suburbs. App cabs work everywhere.

## Day 1: South Mumbai and the Elephanta Caves

### Gateway of India

Built to welcome King George V and Queen Mary in 1911, the arch faces the harbour opposite the famous Taj Mahal Palace hotel. Come early before the crowds.

### Elephanta Caves

Ferries leave from the Gateway of India to Elephanta Island, a ride of about an hour each way. The rock-cut caves, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, date back to around the 5th to 8th centuries and hold the magnificent three-headed **Trimurti** (Maheshmurti) sculpture of Shiva. There is a climb of about 120 steps from the jetty to the caves. The caves are closed on Mondays, and ferries do not run in rough monsoon weather, so plan accordingly. Allow about four to five hours for the round trip.

### Colaba and Kala Ghoda

Back on the mainland, walk **Colaba Causeway** for street shopping and Leopold Café, then **Kala Ghoda**, Mumbai's arts district, with galleries, the **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya** museum and the Jehangir Art Gallery.

### Victorian and Art Deco Mumbai

The Victorian Gothic and Art Deco Ensembles of Mumbai are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. See the Bombay High Court, Mumbai University's Rajabai Clock Tower, and the Art Deco buildings along the Oval Maidan, where you will often see a cricket match in progress.

### Evening: Marine Drive

Walk Marine Drive, the "Queen's Necklace", at sunset, and continue to **Girgaon Chowpatty** for bhel puri, pav bhaji and kulfi on the beach.

## Day 2: CSMT, Haji Ali and Bandra

### Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (CSMT)

A breathtaking Victorian Gothic railway station and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, still one of the busiest stations in India. Look up at the domes and gargoyles from across the road.

### Crawford Market and Mohammed Ali Road

Crawford Market is a covered market with fruit, spices and household goods. During Ramzan, the food stalls of Mohammed Ali Road come alive after sunset.

### Dhobi Ghat

The world's largest open-air laundry, best seen from the Mahalaxmi station bridge.

### Haji Ali Dargah and Mahalakshmi Temple

The white Haji Ali Dargah sits on an islet in the sea, reached by a causeway that is only accessible at low tide, so check the tide times. The Mahalakshmi Temple is nearby.

### Siddhivinayak Temple

Mumbai's most famous Ganesh temple, in Prabhadevi. Tuesdays are the busiest.

### Bandra

Cross the **Bandra–Worli Sea Link** to Bandra. Walk along **Bandstand** and **Carter Road**, see Mount Mary Church and the colourful streets of Ranwar and Chapel Road, and have dinner at one of Bandra's cafés.

## If you have more time

- **Sanjay Gandhi National Park and the Kanheri Caves:** a forest inside the city, with over 100 Buddhist caves.
- **Juhu Beach:** sunset and street food in the western suburbs.
- **A guided Dharavi tour:** learn about the small industries and community life in one of Asia's largest informal settlements. Choose a responsible tour that does not allow photography of residents.
- **A Bollywood studio tour** at Film City.

## What to eat in Mumbai

- **Vada pav:** Mumbai's signature snack.
- **Pav bhaji** and **misal pav**.
- **Bhel puri** and **sev puri** at Chowpatty and Juhu.
- **Bombil fry**, **surmai** and Malvani seafood.
- **Irani café** classics: bun maska, chai and berry pulao.
- **Kulfi** and **falooda**.

## Best time to visit Mumbai

November to February is the most comfortable. The monsoon (June to September) is dramatic and green but brings heavy rain, flooding and cancelled Elephanta ferries. **Ganesh Chaturthi** (August or September) is the city's biggest festival, spectacular but extremely crowded.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 2 days enough for Mumbai?

Yes, for the highlights of South Mumbai, Elephanta and Bandra. Add a third day for the national park, Juhu or a day trip.

### Can I visit the Elephanta Caves in the monsoon?

Ferries are often suspended in rough weather during the monsoon. Check before you go.

### Are Mumbai's local trains safe for tourists?

Yes, outside rush hours. Keep your belongings close, avoid peak times and consider first class.
