Mathura and Vrindavan, in the Braj region of Uttar Pradesh, are the land of Lord Krishna. Mathura is where he was born, and Vrindavan is where he spent his childhood. Together with Govardhan, Barsana, Nandgaon and Gokul they form one of India's most joyful pilgrim circuits, famous for temple music, butter sweets and the most colourful Holi in the country.

## How to reach Mathura and Vrindavan

- **By road:** Mathura is about 180 km from Delhi on the Yamuna Expressway, around 3 hours by car, and about 60 km from Agra. Vrindavan is about 12 km from Mathura.
- **By train:** Mathura Junction is on the Delhi–Mumbai and Delhi–Chennai lines, with many fast trains from Delhi.
- **By air:** Delhi and Agra are the nearest practical airports.

This makes Braj an easy add-on to an Agra trip. See our [Agra and Taj Mahal guide](/blog/agra-taj-mahal-guide).

## Mathura: temples and ghats

### Shri Krishna Janmabhoomi

The temple complex built around the spot believed to be Krishna's birthplace, including the prison-cell shrine (Garbha Griha). Security is strict: phones, bags and cameras are not allowed, so leave them in your vehicle or the cloakroom.

### Dwarkadhish Temple

A busy temple in the old city, known for its beautiful paintings and the swing (jhula) festival during the monsoon month of Shravan.

### Vishram Ghat

The most important of Mathura's ghats on the Yamuna, where Krishna is said to have rested after defeating Kansa. The evening Yamuna aarti here is lovely, and you can take a short boat ride along the river.

## Vrindavan: temples you should not miss

### Banke Bihari Temple

The most loved temple in Vrindavan. The curtain in front of the idol is drawn every few minutes, as devotees believe no one should gaze at Banke Bihari too long. The lanes to the temple are narrow and crowds can be intense, especially on weekends and festivals, so hold on to children and older family members.

### ISKCON Vrindavan (Krishna Balaram Mandir)

A clean, well-organised temple with continuous kirtan. It is popular with international visitors and has a good vegetarian restaurant.

### Prem Mandir

A white marble temple set in large gardens, with tableaux of Krishna's life. It is especially beautiful in the evening when the whole building is lit with changing coloured lights.

### Radha Raman, Radha Vallabh and Nidhivan

Radha Raman is one of the oldest and most revered temples. Nidhivan is a sacred grove where, according to belief, Krishna still performs the Raas Leela at night, which is why it is closed after dark.

## The Govardhan Parikrama

Govardhan Hill, about 22 km from Mathura, is the hill Krishna is said to have lifted to protect the people of Braj from Indra's rains. Devotees walk the **parikrama** around it, a circuit of about 21 km that takes most people five to seven hours on foot. Some do it barefoot; others do it by e-rickshaw.

**Tips:** start before dawn, carry water, wear comfortable footwear if you choose to, and break at Radha Kund and Kusum Sarovar. The parikrama is busiest on Purnima (full moon) days and around Guru Purnima.

## Holi in Braj

Holi in Braj stretches over more than a week before the main festival:

- **Lathmar Holi in Barsana and Nandgaon:** the women of Barsana playfully beat the men of Nandgaon with sticks, recalling Krishna's visit to Radha's village. It takes place a few days before Holi.
- **Phoolon wali Holi (flower Holi) in Vrindavan:** usually around Rangbhari Ekadashi, flowers rather than colours are showered on devotees at Banke Bihari.
- **Widows' Holi in Vrindavan:** in recent years, widows living in Vrindavan's ashrams have celebrated Holi together, a moving break from old taboos.
- **Holi day and Huranga at Dauji:** the main colour festival, followed by the energetic Huranga at the Dauji temple near Baldeo.

**Holi tips:** dates follow the lunar calendar, so check the Braj Holi schedule each year. Wear old clothes, protect your phone and eyes, travel in a group and book stays well in advance.

## Food to try in Braj

Mathura is famous for its **pedas**. Also try kachori-sabzi and jalebi for breakfast, lassi in clay cups, makhan-mishri (butter and sugar crystals, Krishna's favourite) and the simple temple prasad. Food in the temple towns is vegetarian and usually without onion and garlic.

## Suggested two-day plan

**Day 1: Vrindavan.** Banke Bihari in the morning, then Radha Raman and Nidhivan, ISKCON after lunch, and Prem Mandir at dusk.

**Day 2: Mathura and Govardhan.** An early Govardhan parikrama (or a drive to Radha Kund), then Krishna Janmabhoomi and Dwarkadhish in the afternoon, and the evening aarti at Vishram Ghat.

## Best time to visit

October to March is pleasant. Holi (February or March) and Janmashtami (August or September) are the biggest festivals, with very large crowds. Summers are extremely hot.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days are enough for Mathura and Vrindavan?

Two days covers the main temples and a short Govardhan visit. Add a day for Barsana and Nandgaon, or for Holi.

### Can I visit Mathura and Vrindavan as a day trip from Delhi or Agra?

Yes, but it is rushed. From Agra, a day trip is comfortable. From Delhi, stay at least one night.

### What is the best place to experience Holi in Braj?

For the most famous experience, go to Barsana for Lathmar Holi and Vrindavan's Banke Bihari temple for flower Holi.
