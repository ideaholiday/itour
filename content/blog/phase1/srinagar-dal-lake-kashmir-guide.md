Kashmir has been called paradise on earth for centuries, and Srinagar is the best place to start. Built around Dal Lake and the Jhelum river, the city has painted wooden houseboats, Mughal gardens, old mosques and shrines, and the snow-capped Pir Panjal and Zabarwan ranges all around it. From Srinagar, the meadows of Gulmarg, Pahalgam and Sonamarg are each a day trip or a short stay away.

## How to reach Srinagar

- **By air:** Srinagar International Airport has direct flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru and other cities. This is by far the easiest way in.
- **By train:** Rail links into the Kashmir valley have expanded in recent years. Check the latest services from Jammu and Katra before you plan around the train.
- **By road:** Jammu to Srinagar is about 250 km on the national highway. Travel time varies a lot with weather and road work.

**Before you travel:** check current travel advisories and road status for Jammu and Kashmir, and carry a government photo ID at all times.

## Dal Lake: shikaras and houseboats

A **shikara ride** at sunset is the classic Srinagar experience. Boatmen take you past floating gardens, lotus beds, Char Chinar island and the lake's markets, where vendors paddle up selling saffron, kahwa and handicrafts. Rates are set by the tourism authorities for set routes and durations, so ask for the official rate card at the ghat.

**Stay a night on a houseboat.** Srinagar's houseboats date back to the British era, when outsiders were not allowed to own land. They range from simple to very luxurious, with carved walnut ceilings and deep sofas. Houseboats on **Nigeen Lake** are usually quieter than those on the busier parts of Dal.

**The floating vegetable market** takes place very early in the morning, around dawn. Ask your houseboat owner to arrange a shikara.

## The Mughal gardens

- **Nishat Bagh:** the largest, with twelve terraces rising from the lake to the mountains.
- **Shalimar Bagh:** built by Emperor Jahangir for Nur Jahan, with fountains, chinar trees and black marble pavilions.
- **Chashme Shahi:** a small, lovely garden around a natural spring.
- **Pari Mahal:** a former Sufi school on a hillside, with the best views over Dal Lake, especially at sunset.

## The Tulip Garden

The **Indira Gandhi Memorial Tulip Garden** below the Zabarwan hills is one of the largest tulip gardens in Asia. It opens for only a few weeks in spring, usually from late March to late April, when millions of tulips bloom in rows of colour. Check the opening dates each year and go early in the morning to avoid crowds.

## Mosques, shrines and the old city

- **Hazratbal Shrine:** a white marble shrine on the banks of Dal Lake, revered for housing a relic of the Prophet.
- **Jamia Masjid:** a vast wooden mosque with 370 deodar pillars in the old city.
- **Shankaracharya Temple:** an ancient Shiva temple on a hill above the city, with panoramic views. Phones and cameras may not be allowed.
- **Downtown Srinagar:** walk along the Jhelum to see old wooden houses, the Khanqah-e-Moula shrine and craftsmen making papier-mâché, carpets and copperware.

## Day trips and short stays

### Gulmarg

About 50 km from Srinagar, Gulmarg is a vast meadow ringed by pine forests. The **Gulmarg Gondola** is one of the highest cable cars in the world, rising in two phases towards Apharwat peak. In winter, Gulmarg is India's best-known ski destination; in summer, it is ideal for pony rides and walks. Book gondola tickets online in advance during peak season.

### Pahalgam

About 90 km away, in the Lidder valley. See Betaab Valley, Aru Valley and Chandanwari, raft on the Lidder river or ride ponies to Baisaran. Pahalgam is also the base for the Amarnath Yatra in summer.

### Sonamarg

About 80 km away on the road to Ladakh, "the meadow of gold" leads to the Thajiwas Glacier, reached on foot or by pony. From here the road continues over Zoji La to Ladakh; see our [Ladakh travel guide](/blog/ladakh-complete-travel-guide).

## Kashmiri food to try

Kashmiri **Wazwan** is a multi-course feast: rogan josh, yakhni, rista, gushtaba and tabak maaz. Vegetarians should try dum aloo, nadru (lotus stem) and haak saag. Drink **kahwa**, saffron and almond green tea, and try the salty pink **noon chai** with a fresh girda bread from a local bakery.

## Best time to visit

- **Spring (March to May):** tulips, blossoms and pleasant weather.
- **Summer (June to August):** green meadows and cool days; the busiest season.
- **Autumn (September to November):** golden chinar leaves and clear skies. Many people's favourite.
- **Winter (December to February):** snow, skiing in Gulmarg and a frozen, magical feel, but very cold.

## A 5-day Kashmir plan

1. Arrive in Srinagar; sunset shikara on Dal Lake; night on a houseboat.
2. Mughal gardens, Pari Mahal and the old city.
3. Day trip to Gulmarg.
4. Pahalgam (stay a night if you can).
5. Sonamarg, or a slow morning in Srinagar before your flight.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to travel to Kashmir?

Millions of tourists visit Kashmir every year. Check current advisories before you travel, follow local guidance and book with registered operators.

### How many days are enough for Kashmir?

Five to seven days covers Srinagar, Gulmarg, Pahalgam and Sonamarg.

### Should I stay in a hotel or a houseboat?

Try at least one night on a houseboat for the experience, then choose based on comfort. Houseboats on Nigeen Lake are usually quieter.
