Ayodhya, on the banks of the Saryu river in Uttar Pradesh, is one of the seven sacred cities of Hinduism and the birthplace of Lord Ram. Since the Pran Pratishtha of the Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Mandir in January 2024, it has become one of the most visited pilgrim towns in India. This guide covers how to plan a smooth darshan, what else to see in the old town, and how to fit Ayodhya into a longer trip through Uttar Pradesh.

## How to reach Ayodhya

- **By air:** Maharishi Valmiki International Airport, Ayodhya Dham, is a short drive from the temple area. Lucknow's Chaudhary Charan Singh Airport has many more flights and is about three hours away by road.
- **By train:** Ayodhya Dham Junction and Ayodhya Cantt (formerly Faizabad) stations are both well connected. Express trains, including Vande Bharat services, link Ayodhya with Lucknow, Delhi and other major cities.
- **By road:** Lucknow is roughly 135 km away and Varanasi about 200 km. A private cab is the most comfortable option for families and senior citizens.

## Ram Mandir darshan: what to know before you go

The temple is built in the Nagara style from pink sandstone, with carved pillars and a sanctum that holds the idol of Ram Lalla. A few practical points make the visit much easier:

- **Carry as little as possible.** Mobile phones, cameras, bags, electronic items and leather goods are not allowed inside. Use the cloakroom and locker facilities near the entry.
- **Go early.** Queues are shortest soon after the temple opens in the morning. Weekends, public holidays, Ram Navami and the Diwali period are the busiest.
- **Check timings and aarti passes on the official Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Teerth Kshetra channels** before you travel, as darshan and aarti timings change with the season.
- **Wear comfortable footwear you can leave behind** and dress modestly. Expect a good amount of walking between the drop-off point and the temple.
- **Senior citizens and people with disabilities** may be offered wheelchairs and separate assistance; ask at the help desk.

A calm darshan usually takes two to three hours from the security check to the exit on a normal day, and longer on festival days.

## Other temples in Ayodhya worth visiting

### Hanuman Garhi

A tall, fort-like temple reached by about 76 steps, dedicated to Hanuman. Tradition says devotees should visit Hanuman Garhi before the Ram Mandir. Mornings are crowded but lively, with the smell of fresh laddoos from the shops below.

### Kanak Bhawan

Believed to have been gifted to Sita by Kaikeyi, Kanak Bhawan is known for its golden-crowned idols of Ram and Sita and its peaceful courtyard. Devotional singing here in the evening is a highlight.

### Dashrath Mahal and Nageshwarnath Temple

Dashrath Mahal is linked to King Dashrath's royal palace, while the Nageshwarnath Temple, dedicated to Shiva, is one of the oldest shrines in the town and very busy during Mahashivratri.

## The Saryu ghats

- **Ram ki Paidi:** a series of ghats and steps along the Saryu that glow at night. This is the centre of Ayodhya's Deepotsav, when lakhs of diyas are lit on the eve of Diwali.
- **Naya Ghat and the Saryu aarti:** the evening aarti on the Saryu is simpler than the Ganga aarti in Varanasi but deeply moving. Arrive before sunset to get a good spot.
- **Guptar Ghat:** a quieter ghat a short drive away, traditionally where Lord Ram is said to have taken Jal Samadhi. Good for a peaceful morning walk.

A short boat ride on the Saryu at sunrise or sunset is one of the best ways to see the town.

## Suggested one-day and two-day plans

**One day in Ayodhya**

1. Early morning: Hanuman Garhi, then Ram Mandir darshan.
2. Late morning: Kanak Bhawan and Dashrath Mahal.
3. Afternoon: lunch and rest.
4. Evening: Saryu aarti and a walk along Ram ki Paidi.

**Two days in Ayodhya**

Add Guptar Ghat, Nageshwarnath Temple, the Tulsi Smarak Bhawan museum and a sunrise boat ride on the second day, at a slower pace.

## Best time to visit Ayodhya

October to March is the most comfortable, with cool mornings and pleasant days. Diwali brings the spectacular Deepotsav but also very large crowds, so book stays early. Ram Navami (March or April) is the most important festival here. Summer (April to June) is very hot, and the monsoon months can be humid.

## Where to stay and what to eat

Ayodhya now has a growing mix of hotels, dharamshalas, homestays and tent-city style stays, with more options across the river and in Faizabad. Book early for festival dates. Food in the temple town is sattvik and vegetarian: try kachori-sabzi for breakfast, pedas and laddoos from Hanuman Garhi, and a simple thali at a local bhojnalaya.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days are enough for Ayodhya?

One full day covers the Ram Mandir, Hanuman Garhi, Kanak Bhawan and the evening aarti. Two days lets you see the town at an easy pace.

### Can I carry my phone to the Ram Mandir?

No. Phones and electronic items are not allowed inside. Deposit them at the cloakroom near the entry.

### Is Ayodhya suitable for senior citizens?

Yes, with planning. Go early, use the assistance facilities and wheelchairs, and hire a private cab for travel between sites.

### What is the best way to travel from Lucknow to Ayodhya?

A private cab takes about three hours and is the most flexible option. Trains are also frequent and comfortable.
