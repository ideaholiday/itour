Kedarnath, at about 3,583 metres in the Garhwal Himalayas, is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas of Shiva and one of the four shrines of the Char Dham Yatra in Uttarakhand. The stone temple, framed by snow peaks and the Mandakini river, is reached only on foot, by pony or palki, or by helicopter. This guide covers everything you need to plan a safe, well-paced yatra.

## When is Kedarnath open?

The temple is open for about six months a year. The opening date is announced each year and usually falls around **Akshaya Tritiya (late April or May)**, and the temple closes around **Bhai Dooj (October or November)**, after which the deity is taken to Ukhimath for the winter.

- **May and June:** the busiest months, with long queues.
- **July to mid-September:** the monsoon brings landslides and route closures. Travel only if you are flexible, and check weather alerts.
- **Mid-September to October:** clear skies, fewer crowds and cold nights. Many regular pilgrims prefer this window.

## Registration is mandatory

Every pilgrim must register for the Char Dham Yatra, including Kedarnath, on the **official Uttarakhand tourism registration portal** or its app before travelling. Registration is checked on the way. Carry the registration slip and a government photo ID.

## How to reach Kedarnath

The route runs **Haridwar or Rishikesh → Rudraprayag → Guptkashi → Sonprayag → Gaurikund**. Rishikesh to Sonprayag is a long day's drive of roughly 8 to 10 hours on mountain roads.

- Private vehicles usually park at **Sonprayag**, and shared jeeps take pilgrims the last few kilometres to **Gaurikund**, where the trek starts.
- The nearest airport is Jolly Grant, Dehradun, and the nearest major railhead is Rishikesh or Haridwar.
- Stay overnight at Guptkashi, Sitapur or Sonprayag before the trek so you can start at dawn.

## The trek from Gaurikund

The trail from Gaurikund to Kedarnath is about **16 to 18 km** one way, with a steady climb of over 1,500 metres. It is paved and well marked, with shelters, water points, medical posts and food stalls along the way.

- **On foot:** most fit walkers take 6 to 9 hours up and 5 to 6 hours down.
- **By pony or mule:** book only from official counters at Gaurikund, at the rates displayed.
- **By palki (doli) or pithu:** carried by porters; useful for elderly pilgrims. Again, use official counters.

Main stops include **Jungle Chatti, Bhimbali, Linchauli** and **Base Camp**, before the final walk to the temple.

## Helicopter tickets: book only on the official site

Helicopter services fly from helipads around **Phata, Sersi and Guptkashi** to Kedarnath in a few minutes. Tickets are sold only through the **official IRCTC Heli Yatra booking website**, and slots open in advance and sell out fast.

Fake helicopter booking websites and social media pages are a well-known scam on this route. Never pay an agent or a page that promises "confirmed" helicopter tickets outside the official site.

Helicopter flights depend on the weather and are often delayed or cancelled in bad visibility, so keep a spare day.

## At the temple

- **Darshan:** queues can take several hours in peak season. Arrive early and dress warmly.
- **Bhim Shila:** the large boulder behind the temple that is believed to have shielded it during the 2013 floods.
- **Adi Shankaracharya's Samadhi:** rebuilt behind the temple, with a statue of the saint.
- **Bhairavnath Temple:** a short climb above Kedarnath with wide views of the valley.
- **Evening aarti:** simple and powerful in the cold mountain air.

Stays at Kedarnath range from GMVN huts and tents to basic lodges. Book ahead in peak season.

## Altitude and health tips

- **Acclimatise:** spend a night at Guptkashi or Sonprayag, and walk slowly.
- **Know the signs of altitude sickness:** headache, nausea, dizziness and breathlessness. Descend and see a medical post if symptoms get worse.
- **Consult your doctor** if you have heart, lung or blood-pressure conditions. Some pilgrims are advised to get a medical check-up before the yatra.
- **Drink water**, avoid alcohol and eat light meals.

## What to pack

- Warm layers, a waterproof jacket and a rain poncho, even in summer.
- Good walking shoes with grip, plus woollen socks.
- A walking stick, torch and power bank.
- Basic medicines, including ORS and anything your doctor recommends for altitude.
- Cash, as ATMs and networks are unreliable higher up.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long is the Kedarnath trek?

About 16 to 18 km one way from Gaurikund. Most people walk up in one day and come down the next.

### Can senior citizens do the Kedarnath yatra?

Yes, many do, using a helicopter, pony or palki. Consult a doctor first and plan extra rest days.

### How many days are needed for Kedarnath from Haridwar?

Plan four to five days: one to reach Guptkashi or Sonprayag, one to trek up, one for darshan and the return trek, and one to drive back.

### Is registration required for Kedarnath?

Yes. Registration on the official Uttarakhand portal is mandatory for all pilgrims.
