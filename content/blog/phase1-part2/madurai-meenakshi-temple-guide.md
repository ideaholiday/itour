Madurai is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in India, a centre of Tamil culture for more than two thousand years. At its heart stands the **Meenakshi Amman Temple**, with towering gopurams covered in thousands of painted figures. The whole old city is laid out in concentric streets around it, named after Tamil months, and the temple's rhythm still shapes daily life.

## How to reach Madurai

- **By air:** Madurai Airport has flights from Chennai, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi and Hyderabad.
- **By train:** Madurai Junction is well connected with Chennai, Bengaluru, Rameswaram and Kanyakumari.
- **By road:** Rameswaram is about 170 km away and Kodaikanal about 120 km.

## The Meenakshi Amman Temple

The temple is dedicated to **Meenakshi**, a form of Parvati, and her consort **Sundareswarar** (Shiva). The present complex was largely built by the Nayak rulers in the 16th and 17th centuries.

**What to see:**

- **The gopurams:** fourteen gateway towers, the tallest (the southern gopuram) about 52 metres high and covered in brightly painted sculptures of gods, demons and animals.
- **The Golden Lotus Tank (Potramarai Kulam):** where the ancient Tamil Sangam poets are said to have gathered.
- **The Hall of Thousand Pillars:** a hall of intricately carved pillars that now houses the temple art museum. The **musical pillars** nearby produce different notes when struck.
- **The night ceremony (Palliyarai Pooja):** every night, Sundareswarar is carried in a palanquin to Meenakshi's shrine with music and lamps. It is one of the most atmospheric rituals in South India.

**Rules and tips:**

- **Phones and cameras are not allowed inside.** Deposit them at the counters near the entrances.
- **Dress code:** cover shoulders and knees; no shorts. Men may be asked to wear a dhoti in some inner areas.
- **Non-Hindus** can visit most of the temple, but the inner sanctums are for Hindus only.
- **Special darshan tickets** are available for shorter queues.
- **Go early in the morning or for the night ceremony,** when the temple is most alive.

## Thirumalai Nayak Palace

Built in 1636 by King Thirumalai Nayak, the palace combines Dravidian and Islamic styles, with giant white columns and a grand courtyard. Only part of the original survives. The evening sound and light show tells the story of the Nayak dynasty and the epic of Silappathikaram.

## Gandhi Memorial Museum

Housed in the 17th-century Tamukkam Palace, it tells the story of India's freedom struggle and holds the blood-stained dhoti Gandhi was wearing when he was assassinated. It was in Madurai, in 1921, that Gandhi first adopted the simple loincloth.

## Other places worth visiting

- **Vandiyur Mariamman Teppakulam:** a huge temple tank that hosts the **Float Festival** in January or February, when the deities are floated on illuminated rafts.
- **Thiruparankundram:** one of the six abodes of Murugan, a rock-cut temple about 8 km away.
- **Alagar Kovil:** a Vishnu temple in the hills, about 20 km away.
- **Puthu Mandapam and the textile market:** tailors, bronze and brass, and Madurai's famous **Sungudi cotton sarees**.

## Festivals

The **Chithirai Festival** (April or May) re-enacts the celestial wedding of Meenakshi and Sundareswarar, followed by the procession of Lord Kallazhagar entering the Vaigai river. It draws enormous crowds and is the city's biggest event.

## What to eat in Madurai

Madurai is one of Tamil Nadu's great food cities and famously eats late into the night.

- **Jigarthanda:** the city's signature cold drink of milk, almond gum, rose syrup and ice cream.
- **Kari dosa:** a thick dosa topped with egg and spiced minced mutton.
- **Bun parotta** and **kothu parotta** with salna.
- **Idli and dosa breakfasts** with many chutneys.
- **Paruthi paal:** a sweet cotton-seed milk drink.
- **Filter coffee**, everywhere.

## Best time to visit

October to March is the most comfortable. The Chithirai Festival (April or May) and the Float Festival (January or February) are worth planning for. Summers are very hot.

## Day trips from Madurai

- **Rameswaram:** the Ramanathaswamy Temple with its famous corridors, and the Pamban Bridge.
- **Kodaikanal:** a hill station about 3 hours away.
- **Chettinad:** mansions and famous cuisine around Karaikudi.
- **Kanyakumari:** India's southern tip, about 250 km away.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much time do I need at the Meenakshi Temple?

Two to three hours for a relaxed visit. Come back at night for the Palliyarai ceremony if you can.

### Can I carry my phone inside the Meenakshi Temple?

No. Phones and cameras must be deposited at the counters outside.

### How many days are enough for Madurai?

One to two days for the city, plus a day for Rameswaram or Kodaikanal.
