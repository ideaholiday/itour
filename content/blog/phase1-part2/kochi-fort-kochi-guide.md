Kochi (Cochin) has been a port for spices for centuries, and its history shows in its streets: Chinese fishing nets on the waterfront, a Portuguese-era church, a Dutch palace, a 16th-century synagogue and old spice warehouses turned into cafés and galleries. It is also the easiest gateway to Kerala's backwaters and hills, and a lovely place to start any Kerala trip.

## How to reach Kochi

- **By air:** Cochin International Airport has flights from all major Indian cities and the Gulf. It is about 1 to 1.5 hours from Fort Kochi by road.
- **By train:** Ernakulam Junction and Ernakulam Town are the main stations.
- **Getting to Fort Kochi:** from Ernakulam, take a taxi, or the ferry or **Kochi Water Metro** across the harbour for a scenic crossing.

## Fort Kochi

Fort Kochi is the heart of old Kochi, best explored on foot or by bicycle.

### Chinese fishing nets

The giant cantilevered fishing nets along the waterfront are thought to have been introduced by traders from the court of Kublai Khan. They are worked by teams of fishermen and are most photogenic at sunset. Fishermen often let visitors help lift the net for a small tip.

### St Francis Church

One of the oldest European-built churches in India. **Vasco da Gama** was originally buried here in 1524 before his remains were taken back to Portugal; the gravestone is still inside.

### Santa Cruz Cathedral Basilica

A white Gothic basilica with painted ceilings and stained glass, a short walk from St Francis Church.

### Walks and art

Walk along Princess Street and Burgher Street, see the Dutch Cemetery and the old bungalows, and visit the galleries and cafés in restored warehouses. In **Biennale years**, the **Kochi-Muziris Biennale** (usually December to March) fills Fort Kochi with contemporary art.

## Mattancherry and Jew Town

### Mattancherry Palace (Dutch Palace)

Built by the Portuguese and later renovated by the Dutch, the palace has some of the finest **Kerala murals** in the state, showing scenes from the Ramayana and the Puranas.

### Paradesi Synagogue

Built in **1568**, it is one of the oldest active synagogues in the Commonwealth, with hand-painted Chinese floor tiles and Belgian chandeliers. It is **closed on Fridays, Saturdays and Jewish holidays**, so plan around that.

### Jew Town

The lanes around the synagogue are full of antique shops, spice sellers and handicraft stores. The old spice warehouses still trade pepper, cardamom and ginger.

## Kathakali and Kalaripayattu

Watch a **Kathakali** performance, Kerala's classical dance-drama, at one of Fort Kochi's cultural centres. Arrive early to watch the artists apply their elaborate make-up, which is a performance in itself. Many centres also host **Kalaripayattu**, Kerala's ancient martial art.

## Ernakulam and beyond

- **Marine Drive:** a waterfront promenade in Ernakulam, lovely at sunset.
- **Hill Palace Museum, Tripunithura:** the former royal palace of the Kochi rulers.
- **Cherai Beach:** a quieter beach about 25 km north.
- **Kumbalangi:** a model tourism village for a slow day on the backwaters.

## What to eat in Kochi

- **Karimeen pollichathu:** pearl spot fish cooked in banana leaf.
- **Kerala fish curry** and **prawn roast**.
- **Appam with stew**, a classic Syrian Christian breakfast.
- **Kerala sadya** on a banana leaf, especially at Onam.
- **Beef fry and parotta**, a Kerala favourite.
- **Banana chips** and **filter coffee**.

## Best time to visit

October to March is the most pleasant. The monsoon (June to September) is lush and good for Ayurveda, but rain is heavy. **Onam** (August or September) is Kerala's biggest festival.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days are enough for Kochi?

Two days covers Fort Kochi, Mattancherry, Jew Town and a Kathakali show.

### Should I stay in Fort Kochi or Ernakulam?

Stay in Fort Kochi for heritage and atmosphere, or Ernakulam for convenience, shopping and train connections.

### When is the Paradesi Synagogue closed?

On Fridays, Saturdays and Jewish holidays. Check timings before you go.
