The Andaman Islands have the best scuba diving in India: warm, clear water, healthy coral reefs, turtles, reef sharks and huge schools of fish. **Havelock Island (Swaraj Dweep)** is the diving capital, with dozens of dive centres, while **Neil Island (Shaheed Dweep)** is quieter. You don't need any experience to try; many travellers take their first breath underwater here.

For the rest of your Andaman trip, including Port Blair and Radhanagar Beach, see our [Andaman Islands travel guide](/blog/andaman-islands-travel-guide).

## Getting to Havelock and Neil

Fly to **Port Blair** from Chennai, Kolkata, Delhi, Bengaluru or other cities. From Port Blair, private ferries reach Havelock in about 1.5 to 2.5 hours, and Neil in a similar time. Government ferries are cheaper but sell out fast. Book ferries in advance in peak season.

## Try dive or certification course?

### Discover Scuba Diving (try dive)

- For complete beginners; **no swimming skills are strictly required**, though you should be comfortable in water.
- After a briefing and practice in shallow water, you dive with an instructor, usually to a maximum of about 12 metres.
- The whole experience takes a few hours, with 30 to 45 minutes underwater.
- Minimum age is usually **10 years**.

### Open Water Diver course

- An internationally recognised certification (PADI or SSI) that lets you dive with a buddy to **18 metres** anywhere in the world.
- Takes about **3 to 4 days**, with theory, confined water sessions and four open-water dives.
- You need to pass a basic swimming and floating test.

### Advanced and specialty courses

Once certified, you can take the Advanced Open Water course (to 30 metres), and specialties like deep diving, night diving, and Nitrox.

## The best dive sites

**Around Havelock:**

- **Nemo Reef:** shallow and calm, full of clownfish; popular for try dives.
- **The Aquarium:** colourful coral and lots of reef fish.
- **Lighthouse and Seduction Point:** good for beginners and fun dives.
- **Dixon's Pinnacle:** pinnacles covered in soft coral, with big schools of fish; for certified divers.
- **The Wall:** a sloping wall with sea fans, turtles and sometimes reef sharks.
- **Johnny's Gorge and Jackson's Bar:** advanced sites with strong currents and big marine life.

**Around Neil:** **Bus Stop**, **Margherita's Reef** and **K Reef** offer quieter dives and good coral.

## Snorkelling and sea walking

If you don't want to dive, you can still see the reef:

- **Elephant Beach** (Havelock) has shallow coral close to shore, reached by boat or a jungle walk.
- **Bharatpur Beach** and **Natural Bridge** (Neil) have good shallow reefs at low tide.
- **Sea walking** at Elephant Beach lets you walk on the seabed wearing a helmet, without any diving skills.

## When to dive in the Andamans

- **November to April:** calm seas and the best visibility, often 15 to 30 metres. The best season.
- **May:** diving continues, but pre-monsoon weather can arrive.
- **June to September:** the southwest monsoon brings rough seas; many dive centres close or dive only on calm days.

## Choosing a safe dive centre

- Choose centres affiliated with **PADI or SSI**, with certified instructors.
- Ask about group size; for try dives, **one instructor for one or two divers** is best.
- Check that the equipment looks well maintained and that there is oxygen and first aid on the boat.
- Read recent reviews and ask how long the centre has operated.

## Health and safety rules

- **Fill in the medical questionnaire honestly.** Asthma, heart conditions, recent surgery, ear problems, pregnancy and some medications may mean you need a doctor's clearance or shouldn't dive.
- **Don't dive with a cold or blocked sinuses;** equalising will be painful and risky.
- **No flying after diving:** the usual guidance is to wait at least **12 hours after a single dive and 18 hours after multiple dives** before flying. Plan your last dive and your flight from Port Blair accordingly; the ferry back also takes time.
- **Don't touch coral or marine life,** and don't stand on the reef.
- Use **reef-safe sunscreen**.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I scuba dive in the Andamans if I can't swim?

Yes, for a try dive with an instructor. You'll need to be comfortable in water. The Open Water course requires a basic swim test.

### Havelock or Neil: where should I dive?

Havelock has more dive centres and sites. Neil is quieter and good for relaxed dives. Many divers do both.

### When is the best time for scuba diving in the Andamans?

November to April, when the sea is calm and visibility is best.

### How long do I need to wait before flying after diving?

At least 12 hours after a single dive and 18 hours after multiple dives, as a general guideline. Follow your dive centre's advice.
