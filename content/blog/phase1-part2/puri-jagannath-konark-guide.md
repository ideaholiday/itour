Puri, on the Bay of Bengal in Odisha, is home to the **Jagannath Temple**, one of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites and the centre of the world-famous **Rath Yatra**. It is also a beach town, a base for the UNESCO-listed **Konark Sun Temple**, and the gateway to Chilika, Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon. Together with Bhubaneswar, Puri and Konark form Odisha's "Golden Triangle".

## How to reach Puri

- **By air:** Biju Patnaik International Airport in Bhubaneswar is about 60 km away, around 1.5 hours by road.
- **By train:** Puri station is the end of the line for direct trains from Kolkata, Delhi, and many other cities.
- **By road:** Bhubaneswar and Konark are both connected by good roads and frequent buses.

## The Jagannath Temple

The 12th-century temple is dedicated to Lord Jagannath, with his brother Balabhadra and sister Subhadra. The wooden idols are replaced in the rare Nabakalebara ceremony, and the temple kitchen is one of the largest in the world.

**Important rules:**

- **Only Hindus are allowed inside the temple.** Other visitors can see the temple from the rooftops of nearby buildings, such as the Raghunandan Library, often for a small donation.
- **Phones, cameras, leather items and bags are not allowed.** Use the deposit counters outside.
- **Dress modestly;** traditional clothes are recommended.
- **Beware of pandas (priests) who demand large sums.** You do not need to hire one for darshan. Agree any fee first if you choose a guide.

**Mahaprasad:** the food cooked in the temple kitchen and offered to Jagannath is sold as Mahaprasad at the **Ananda Bazaar** inside the temple complex. It is cooked in earthen pots on wood fires and is considered deeply sacred.

## The Rath Yatra

Every year in June or July (the month of Ashadha), Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra are pulled through the streets on three huge wooden chariots to the **Gundicha Temple**, about 3 km away, and brought back about nine days later. Hundreds of thousands of devotees pull the ropes. New chariots are built every year.

**Rath Yatra tips:** book stays months in advance, expect heat and humidity, watch from a booked balcony or rooftop if you can, and stay with your group in the crowds.

## Puri beach

**Golden Beach**, a Blue Flag–certified stretch, is cleaner and better managed. The main beach is lively in the evenings with food stalls and camel rides. The sea at Puri has strong currents, so **swim only in marked areas where lifeguards are present**.

## Konark Sun Temple

About 35 km from Puri along the coast, the **Konark Sun Temple** was built in the 13th century by King Narasimhadeva I and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is designed as a giant chariot for the Sun God, with **24 carved stone wheels** and **seven horses**. The wheels are also sundials. The carvings of dancers, musicians, animals and daily life are extraordinary.

Go early morning or late afternoon for the best light, and hire an ASI-approved guide at the gate to understand the carvings. The Konark Dance Festival is held every December.

Along the way, stop at **Chandrabhaga Beach** and the **Ramachandi Temple** where the Kushabhadra river meets the sea.

## Raghurajpur heritage village

About 14 km from Puri, Raghurajpur is a village of artists: almost every house makes **Pattachitra** cloth paintings, palm-leaf engravings, masks and toys. The village is also known for Gotipua dance. Buy directly from the artists and ask them to show you how the colours are made.

## Chilika Lake

About 50 km from Puri, Chilika Lake is famous for **Irrawaddy dolphins** (boats leave from **Satapada**) and, in winter, huge flocks of migratory birds at **Nalabana Bird Sanctuary** and Mangalajodi. Go in the morning, and ask boatmen not to chase the dolphins.

## Bhubaneswar: the temple city

If you have a day, see Bhubaneswar's **Lingaraj Temple** (only Hindus inside; there is a viewing platform), the lovely **Mukteshwar Temple**, the **Udayagiri and Khandagiri caves** and the Odisha State Museum.

## What to eat

Try the temple Mahaprasad (dalma, khechudi and sweets), **khaja** from Puri, **chhena poda** (baked cheese dessert), **rasagola** (Odisha has a GI tag for its rasagola), **dahi bara aloo dum** and fresh seafood by the beach.

## Best time to visit

October to February is the most pleasant. The Rath Yatra (June or July) is extraordinary but hot, humid and crowded. Summers are hot, and the monsoon brings heavy rain and occasional cyclones along the coast.

## A 3-day plan

1. **Day 1:** Jagannath Temple darshan early morning, Ananda Bazaar, Golden Beach in the evening.
2. **Day 2:** Konark Sun Temple and Chandrabhaga Beach, then Raghurajpur on the way back.
3. **Day 3:** Chilika Lake from Satapada, or Bhubaneswar's temples before your flight.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can non-Hindus enter the Jagannath Temple in Puri?

No. Only Hindus are allowed inside. Other visitors can view the temple from nearby rooftops.

### How far is Konark from Puri?

About 35 km, around an hour by road along the coast.

### When is the Puri Rath Yatra?

In June or July every year, on the second day of the bright fortnight of Ashadha. Check the exact date each year.
