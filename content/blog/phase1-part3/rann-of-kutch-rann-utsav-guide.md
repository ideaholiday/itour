The **Great Rann of Kutch** in Gujarat is one of the most surreal landscapes in India: a vast white salt desert that stretches to the horizon, glowing silver under a full moon. Around it lies Kutch, a region of embroidered textiles, mud-walled villages, royal palaces in Bhuj and one of the great cities of the Harappan civilisation. Winter, when the Rann Utsav festival brings a tent city to the edge of the salt, is the time to go.

## How to reach Kutch

- **By air:** Bhuj Airport has flights from Mumbai. Ahmedabad has many more flights and is about 330 km from Bhuj.
- **By train:** Bhuj station has direct trains from Mumbai, Ahmedabad and Delhi.
- **By road:** Ahmedabad to Bhuj takes about 6 to 7 hours. The White Rann at **Dhordo** is about 80 km north of Bhuj.

## The White Rann

The salt flats near **Dhordo** are the most visited part of the Great Rann. After the monsoon, the seasonal water evaporates and leaves a crust of salt that is at its whitest from roughly **November to February**.

- **Sunset and moonrise** are the best times, and **full moon nights** are spectacular.
- A **watchtower** and walkway at the entry point give wide views.
- Camel carts and short rides are available near the entry.

**Permit:** you need a permit to enter the White Rann area, since it is near the international border. It can be obtained online or at the **Bhirandiyara** checkpoint on the way. Carry photo ID.

## Rann Utsav

The **Rann Utsav** is a festival run by Gujarat Tourism, usually from around November to February. It includes a large **tent city at Dhordo** with deluxe and premium tents, cultural performances, folk music, craft stalls and excursions. Book tent stays well in advance, especially around full moons, Christmas and New Year.

If you prefer a quieter stay, **bhunga** (round mud hut) resorts in Dhordo and nearby villages are a lovely alternative.

## Kalo Dungar

**Kalo Dungar (Black Hill)**, the highest point in Kutch at about 460 metres, gives a panoramic view of the Rann stretching towards the border. It has a temple of Dattatreya where jackals are fed prasad every evening. Go for sunset.

## Bhuj

Bhuj, the main town of Kutch, was badly damaged in the 2001 earthquake but has rebuilt beautifully.

- **Aina Mahal:** an 18th-century palace with a hall of mirrors and European-style interiors.
- **Prag Mahal:** a 19th-century Italian-Gothic palace with a clock tower you can climb.
- **Kutch Museum:** the oldest museum in Gujarat.
- **Smritivan:** a memorial and museum to the 2001 earthquake victims, with excellent exhibits.
- **Bhujodi's Vande Mataram Memorial** and the **Shrujan Living and Learning Design Centre** for Kutch embroidery.

## The craft villages of Kutch

Kutch is one of India's richest craft regions. Visit the artisans directly:

- **Bhujodi:** weaving of shawls and textiles.
- **Ajrakhpur:** hand-block **Ajrakh** printing with natural dyes.
- **Nirona:** **Rogan art** (painting with castor-oil paste), bell making and lacquerware.
- **Hodka and Dhordo:** embroidery, mirror work and leather craft.

Buy directly from artisans and cooperatives, and ask before photographing people.

## Dholavira

**Dholavira**, on Khadir Bet island in the Rann, is one of the largest Harappan cities in India and a **UNESCO World Heritage Site** since 2021. You can see its reservoirs, gateways and the famous signboard inscription site. It is a long drive from Bhuj, so plan a full day or stay nearby. The new **Road to Heaven** through the Rann to Dholavira is a scenic drive in itself.

## Mandvi

About 60 km from Bhuj, **Mandvi** has a quiet beach, the **Vijay Vilas Palace**, and a centuries-old shipyard where wooden dhows are still built by hand.

## Best time to visit

- **November to February:** the Rann is white and firm, the weather is cool and the Rann Utsav is on.
- **March:** getting hot but still possible.
- **Monsoon (July to September):** the Rann floods and becomes a marsh, and the White Rann is not accessible.

Nights in the desert are cold in December and January, so carry warm layers.

## A 4-day Kutch plan

1. **Arrive in Bhuj:** Aina Mahal, Prag Mahal and Smritivan.
2. **Craft villages:** Bhujodi, Ajrakhpur and Nirona, then drive to Dhordo for sunset on the White Rann.
3. **Kalo Dungar** and the villages near Dhordo; full moon night on the Rann if the dates line up.
4. **Mandvi** or **Dholavira**, then back to Bhuj.

## What to eat

Try **Kutchi dabeli**, **bajra rotla** with butter and jaggery, **kadhi-khichdi**, and a full **Gujarati thali**. Many tent camps and village stays serve traditional Kutchi meals.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the best time to see the White Rann?

From November to February, ideally around a full moon.

### Do I need a permit for the Rann of Kutch?

Yes, a permit is needed to enter the White Rann area. Get it online or at the Bhirandiyara checkpoint, and carry photo ID.

### How many days are enough for Kutch?

Three to four days covers Bhuj, the White Rann, Kalo Dungar and the craft villages. Add a day for Dholavira or Mandvi.
