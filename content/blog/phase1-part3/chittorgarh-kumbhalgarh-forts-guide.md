The kingdom of **Mewar** in southern Rajasthan is a land of legendary forts and fierce Rajput history. Two of its greatest strongholds, **Chittorgarh** and **Kumbhalgarh**, are part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the **Hill Forts of Rajasthan**, along with Amber, Jaisalmer, Ranthambore and Gagron. Both are easy to visit from Udaipur and make a superb addition to any Rajasthan trip.

Start with our [Udaipur guide](/blog/udaipur-city-of-lakes-guide), then use this one for the forts.

## Chittorgarh Fort

Chittorgarh is one of the largest forts in India, sprawling across a long plateau above the plains, about 115 km from Udaipur. It was the capital of Mewar for centuries and was besieged three times, each time ending in legendary acts of Rajput valour, including the **jauhar** (self-immolation) of the women of the fort.

### What to see

- **Vijay Stambh (Tower of Victory):** a nine-storey tower built by **Rana Kumbha** in the 15th century to celebrate a victory, covered in carvings of Hindu deities. You can climb the narrow internal stairs.
- **Kirti Stambh (Tower of Fame):** an older, 12th-century tower dedicated to the first Jain Tirthankara, Adinath.
- **Rana Kumbha Palace:** the ruins of the main palace, linked to the legends of Rani Padmini and Meera Bai.
- **Padmini Palace:** a palace beside a pool, linked to the story of Rani Padmini.
- **Meera Temple and Kumbha Shyam Temple:** associated with the saint-poet **Meera Bai**.
- **Gaumukh Kund:** a spring-fed tank on the cliff edge.
- **Fateh Prakash Palace:** now a museum.

**Tips:** the fort is huge, with seven gates on the approach road and about 13 km of roads on top. Hire an auto-rickshaw or a car with a guide, and allow at least half a day. The **evening sound and light show** tells the fort's story.

## Kumbhalgarh Fort

About 85 km north of Udaipur, in the Aravalli hills, **Kumbhalgarh** was built by **Rana Kumbha** in the 15th century and is the birthplace of **Maharana Pratap**. It was captured only once in its history.

### What to see

- **The walls:** Kumbhalgarh's walls run for about **36 km** around the hills, among the longest continuous walls in the world. You can walk a section on top of the wall for superb views.
- **Badal Mahal (Palace of Clouds):** the highest point of the fort, with pastel-painted rooms and views over the Aravallis towards the Thar.
- **Temples:** more than 300 old temples, Hindu and Jain, lie within the walls, including the Neelkanth Mahadev Temple.
- **Light and sound show:** held every evening; the lit-up walls are beautiful.

**Kumbhalgarh Wildlife Sanctuary** around the fort is home to leopards, wolves, sloth bears and many birds. Jeep safaris are available.

## Ranakpur Jain Temple

About 50 km from Kumbhalgarh, the 15th-century **Ranakpur Jain Temple** is one of the most beautiful temples in India. Built of white marble, it is famous for its **1,444 carved pillars**, no two of which are said to be alike. Non-Jain visitors are usually allowed in the afternoon; leather items, and in some cases phones and cameras, have restrictions, so check at the entrance. Dress modestly.

## Haldighati

On the road between Udaipur and Kumbhalgarh, **Haldighati** is the site of the famous 1576 battle between Maharana Pratap and the Mughal forces. A museum tells the story of Pratap and his horse **Chetak**.

## How to plan your visit

- **Chittorgarh:** a day trip from Udaipur (about 2 hours each way), or stay overnight to see the light show.
- **Kumbhalgarh and Ranakpur:** one long day trip from Udaipur, or stay at Kumbhalgarh to see the evening show and continue to Ranakpur and Jodhpur the next day.

A good circuit: **Udaipur → Chittorgarh → Udaipur → Kumbhalgarh → Ranakpur → Jodhpur**. See our [Jodhpur guide](/blog/jodhpur-blue-city-guide) for the next stop.

## Best time to visit

October to March is the best time: pleasant days and cool nights. The monsoon (July to September) makes Kumbhalgarh's hills green and misty. Summers (April to June) are very hot for exploring big forts.

## What to eat

Try **dal baati churma**, **gatte ki sabzi**, **ker sangri** and **laal maas** at Rajasthani restaurants, and a cup of masala chai in the fort towns.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I visit Chittorgarh and Kumbhalgarh in one day?

It is possible but very rushed, as they are in different directions from Udaipur. Plan one day for each.

### How long is the wall of Kumbhalgarh Fort?

About 36 km, one of the longest continuous walls in the world.

### Which is better, Chittorgarh or Kumbhalgarh?

Chittorgarh has more history and monuments; Kumbhalgarh has the more dramatic setting and walls. If you can, see both.
