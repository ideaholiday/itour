Spiti, "the middle land" between Tibet and India, is a high-altitude cold desert in Himachal Pradesh: bare brown mountains, turquoise rivers, whitewashed monasteries clinging to cliffs, and some of the highest villages in the world. A road trip through Spiti is one of the great adventures in India, and one of the most rewarding.

## The two routes into Spiti

### Via Shimla and Kinnaur

- **Route:** Shimla → Narkanda → Rampur → Sangla or Kalpa → Nako → Tabo → Kaza.
- **Open:** most of the year, subject to snow and landslides. This is the only route into Spiti in winter.
- **Why take it:** a gradual gain in altitude, which is much better for acclimatisation, and the beautiful Kinnaur valley on the way.

### Via Manali and Kunzum Pass

- **Route:** Manali → Atal Tunnel → Gramphu → Batal → Kunzum Pass (about 4,550 m) → Losar → Kaza.
- **Open:** usually from about **June to October**, when the snow clears from Kunzum Pass.
- **Why take it:** shorter, with Chandratal Lake on the way, but the altitude gain is sudden and the road includes rough stretches and water crossings.

**The best plan is a circuit:** go in via Kinnaur and come out via Manali, so you acclimatise gradually. Our [Manali guide](/blog/manali-adventure-guide) covers the other end.

## Places you should not miss

### Kaza

The main town of Spiti and the base for most trips, with guesthouses, cafés, an ATM and fuel.

### Key Monastery

The largest monastery in Spiti, a jumble of white buildings on a conical hill above the Spiti river. You can join the monks for butter tea and sometimes a prayer session.

### Kibber and Chicham Bridge

**Kibber** is a high village and the base for **snow leopard** tracking trips in winter. The nearby **Chicham Bridge** spans a deep gorge and is one of the highest bridges in Asia.

### Hikkim, Langza and Komic

- **Hikkim:** home to one of the **highest post offices in the world**, at about 4,400 m. Send a postcard home.
- **Langza:** known for its **fossils** from the time the region was under the sea, and a large statue of the Buddha overlooking the valley.
- **Komic:** one of the highest villages in Asia connected by road, with an old monastery.

### Dhankar

The **Dhankar Monastery** sits dramatically on a crumbling ridge above the confluence of the Spiti and Pin rivers. A steep hike leads to **Dhankar Lake**.

### Tabo

The **Tabo Monastery**, founded in **996 CE**, is one of the oldest continuously functioning Buddhist monasteries in the Himalaya, with ancient murals and statues in its mud-brick halls. It is sometimes called the "Ajanta of the Himalayas".

### Pin Valley

A national park known for the snow leopard and ibex, with traditional villages such as Mud and Sagnam.

### Chandratal Lake

On the Manali route, **Chandratal (Moon Lake)** is a crescent-shaped lake at about 4,300 m. Camping near it is regulated, so stay at the permitted campsites and walk to the lake.

### Nako

On the Kinnaur route, a lovely village with a sacred lake and an old monastery.

## A 9-day itinerary

1. **Shimla to Sangla or Kalpa** (Kinnaur).
2. **Sangla and Chitkul**, the last village on the old Hindustan–Tibet road.
3. **To Nako**, via Khab, the confluence of the Spiti and Sutlej.
4. **To Tabo**, and the Tabo Monastery.
5. **To Kaza**, via Dhankar.
6. **Key, Kibber, Chicham.**
7. **Langza, Hikkim, Komic.**
8. **Kaza to Chandratal** via Kunzum Pass.
9. **Chandratal to Manali.**

## Altitude and safety

Most of Spiti lies between **3,500 and 4,500 metres**.

- **Acclimatise gradually**; the Kinnaur route helps a lot.
- **Watch for altitude sickness:** headache, nausea, breathlessness and poor sleep. Rest, drink water, and descend if symptoms worsen.
- **Avoid alcohol** and heavy exertion for the first few days.
- **Drive in daylight only.** Roads have landslide zones and water crossings that are higher in the afternoon.
- **Carry cash**; ATMs are few and not always working.
- **Mobile networks** are limited, with BSNL and Jio working in some places.

Indian nationals don't need a permit for Spiti. Foreign nationals need an Inner Line Permit for some areas near the border; check the current rules.

## Best time to visit

- **June to September:** both routes open (the Manali side from about June), green valleys and pleasant days.
- **October:** clear skies and autumn colours, but cold nights; Kunzum closes as the snow comes.
- **Winter (December to March):** only the Kinnaur route is open, temperatures drop far below freezing, and many guesthouses close. This is the season for snow leopard expeditions from Kibber.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is the better route to Spiti?

Enter via Shimla and Kinnaur for gradual acclimatisation, and exit via Kunzum Pass and Manali when it is open.

### How many days do I need for Spiti?

At least 8 to 10 days for a full circuit from Shimla to Manali.

### Can I visit Spiti in winter?

Yes, via the Kinnaur route only. It is extremely cold, and many services are closed, so go with an experienced local operator.
