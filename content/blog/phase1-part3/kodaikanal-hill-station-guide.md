Kodaikanal, at about 2,100 metres in the **Palani Hills** of Tamil Nadu, is known as the "Princess of Hill Stations". It was founded in the 19th century by American missionaries and British officials escaping the heat of the plains, and it still has a relaxed, old-world feel: a star-shaped lake, pine and eucalyptus forests, misty viewpoints over the plains, and homemade chocolate shops on every corner.

## How to reach Kodaikanal

- **By road:** Kodaikanal is about 120 km from Madurai (around 3.5 hours) and about 170 km from Coimbatore. The road winds up through the ghats with beautiful views.
- **By train:** **Kodaikanal Road** station, about 80 km away, is on the Chennai–Madurai line.
- **By air:** **Madurai** is the nearest airport; Coimbatore and Tiruchirappalli are other options.

## Things to do in Kodaikanal

### Kodai Lake

A man-made, star-shaped lake at the heart of the town, created in 1863. You can go **boating**, **cycle or walk the 5 km path** around it, or ride a horse along the shore. Early morning, when the mist lifts off the water, is the most beautiful time.

### Coaker's Walk

A paved walkway along the edge of a steep slope, with views over the plains on clear days, and sometimes a sea of clouds below. There is a telescope house halfway.

### Bryant Park

A botanical garden next to the lake, with flower beds and a glass house. The annual **flower show** is held here in May.

### Pillar Rocks

Three granite pillars rising about 120 metres, often wrapped in cloud. The viewpoint is part of a circuit of forest-department viewpoints.

### Guna Caves (Devil's Kitchen)

Deep caves made famous by the 1991 Tamil film Guna and the 2024 film Manjummel Boys. For safety, you can't enter the caves themselves; visitors see them from a fenced viewpoint through the tall pine forest.

### Pine Forest and Moir Point

A tall, quiet pine plantation that is a favourite with photographers and film crews, and a viewpoint over the valley nearby.

### Dolphin's Nose and Vattakanal

A rocky ledge jutting over a deep valley, reached by a short trek from the hamlet of **Vattakanal**, which has cafés and homestays popular with backpackers.

### Berijam Lake

A pristine lake deep in the forest, about 20 km away. It is inside a protected area, so you need a **forest department permit**, which is limited daily and usually issued in Kodaikanal town. You may see bison (gaur) along the way.

### Waterfalls

**Silver Cascade**, on the road up from Madurai, and **Bear Shola Falls** and **Fairy Falls** closer to town are best after the monsoon.

## The Kurinji flower

The Palani Hills are famous for the **neelakurinji** shrub, which blooms only **once every 12 years**, turning the hills blue. Its last major bloom in Kodaikanal was in 2018, so the next is expected in about 2030. The **Kurinji Andavar Temple** is dedicated to Murugan and the flower.

## What to eat and buy

- **Homemade chocolates:** Kodaikanal's specialty, sold in dozens of small shops.
- **Eucalyptus oil** and local spices.
- **Hot chai and bajji** at the lake.
- **Plums, pears and avocados** from local orchards, in season.
- **Cheese** from local farms.

## Best time to visit

- **April to June:** the most popular season, when the plains are hot; expect crowds and higher prices, especially in May.
- **September to November:** fewer crowds, green hills and waterfalls after the rains.
- **December to February:** cold, especially at night, but clear days.
- **Monsoon:** misty and rainy; some viewpoints are clouded over.

## A 2-day plan

1. **Day 1:** morning boating or cycling at Kodai Lake, Bryant Park, Coaker's Walk, and Kurinji Andavar Temple at sunset.
2. **Day 2:** the viewpoint circuit (Pillar Rocks, Guna Caves, Pine Forest, Moir Point), and Berijam Lake if you have a permit; evening chocolate shopping.

Add a third day for Vattakanal, Dolphin's Nose and a slow walk through the forests.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days are enough for Kodaikanal?

Two to three days covers the main sights at a relaxed pace.

### Can you go inside the Guna Caves?

No. For safety, visitors can only see the caves from a fenced viewpoint.

### Do I need a permit for Berijam Lake?

Yes. A limited number of forest department permits are issued each day, usually from Kodaikanal town.
