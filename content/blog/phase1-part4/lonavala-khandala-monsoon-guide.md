Lonavala and its twin town Khandala sit in the Sahyadri hills of the Western Ghats, halfway between Mumbai and Pune. For most of the year they are a quick getaway, but in the **monsoon** they are transformed: waterfalls pour off every cliff, the valleys fill with mist, and the hills turn a brilliant green. Add ancient Buddhist caves, Maratha forts and the famous chikki, and it is easy to see why Lonavala is Maharashtra's favourite weekend escape.

## How to reach Lonavala

- **By road:** about 85 km from Mumbai and 65 km from Pune on the Mumbai–Pune Expressway, roughly 2 hours from Mumbai and 1.5 hours from Pune without traffic.
- **By train:** **Lonavala** station is on the Mumbai–Pune line, with many express and local trains.
- **By air:** **Pune** Airport is the closest; Mumbai has more options.

## Viewpoints and waterfalls

- **Tiger's Leap (Tiger Point):** a cliff with a sheer drop into the valley, shaped like a leaping tiger. In the monsoon, waterfalls tumble nearby and clouds roll through the valley.
- **Lion's Point:** another dramatic viewpoint, popular at sunrise and sunset.
- **Duke's Nose:** a cliff shaped like the Duke of Wellington's nose, popular with rock climbers and trekkers.
- **Rajmachi Point:** views of the Rajmachi fort and the valley below.
- **Kune Falls:** one of the tallest waterfalls in the area, near Khandala.

## Caves and forts

- **Karla Caves:** rock-cut Buddhist caves from around the 2nd century BCE, with one of the largest and best-preserved **chaitya halls** in India, with carved pillars and a wooden ribbed roof. The Ekvira Devi temple is next to the caves.
- **Bhaja Caves:** a group of early Buddhist caves across the valley, with stupas and a small waterfall in the monsoon.
- **Lohagad Fort:** an easy to moderate trek to a Maratha hill fort with a long, narrow ridge called **Vinchu Kata** (scorpion's tail). Great views in the monsoon, but slippery.
- **Visapur Fort:** a larger fort next to Lohagad, with steep, rocky trails that become streams in the monsoon.
- **Rajmachi Fort:** a longer trek or drive to twin forts, especially lovely in the monsoon and for fireflies in May and June.

## Pawna Lake

About 30 km from Lonavala, **Pawna Lake** is known for lakeside **camping**, with tents, barbecues and bonfires on weekends. Choose a camp with good reviews and clear safety rules, and stay out of the water at night.

## Monsoon safety

The monsoon is the most beautiful time to visit, but it is also when most accidents happen.

- **Bhushi Dam** and other dams and waterfalls get very crowded in the monsoon, and water levels can rise suddenly. Drownings have happened here, so stay out of fast-flowing water and follow local restrictions. Authorities sometimes restrict access to certain spots in heavy rain.
- **Stay back from cliff edges**, especially at viewpoints in fog.
- **Trek with good shoes** and avoid forts in very heavy rain.
- **Expect traffic jams** on weekends and holidays; leave early.

## What to eat and buy

- **Chikki:** Lonavala's famous brittle made with jaggery and peanuts, sesame, cashews and more. Many shops sell it along the main road.
- **Fudge** in many flavours.
- **Vada pav** and **bhutta** (roasted corn) at the viewpoints in the rain.
- **Misal pav** and Maharashtrian thalis.

## Best time to visit

- **June to September:** the monsoon, green and dramatic; the most popular time.
- **October to February:** pleasant weather, clear views, good for trekking and camping.
- **March to May:** hot, but quieter.

## A 2-day plan

1. **Day 1:** Karla Caves in the morning, Tiger's Leap and Lion's Point, and chikki shopping in the evening.
2. **Day 2:** Lohagad Fort trek, Bhaja Caves on the way back, and Pawna Lake for sunset.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the best time to visit Lonavala?

June to September for the monsoon scenery, or October to February for pleasant weather and treks.

### Is Lonavala good for a day trip from Mumbai or Pune?

Yes. It is about 2 hours from Mumbai and 1.5 hours from Pune, though staying overnight lets you see more.

### Is Bhushi Dam safe?

It can be dangerous when water levels rise in heavy rain. Stay out of fast-flowing water and follow local restrictions.
