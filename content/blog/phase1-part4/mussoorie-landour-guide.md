Mussoorie, at about 2,000 metres above Dehradun, has been called the "Queen of the Hills" since British times. It has a busy Mall Road, views of the Doon Valley on one side and the snow peaks on the other, and above the town, the quiet cantonment of **Landour**, with its old cottages, churches and bakeries. It is one of the easiest hill escapes from Delhi.

## How to reach Mussoorie

- **By train:** **Dehradun** is the nearest railway station, about 35 km away, with fast trains from Delhi including the Shatabdi and Vande Bharat.
- **By air:** Jolly Grant Airport near Dehradun is about 60 km away.
- **By road:** Delhi is about 290 km away, around 6 to 7 hours by road.

Mussoorie is close to Rishikesh and Haridwar; see our [Rishikesh guide](/blog/rishikesh-yoga-adventure-guide) and [Haridwar guide](/blog/haridwar-ganga-aarti-guide).

## Things to do in Mussoorie

### Mall Road

The main promenade, running from Library Chowk to Picture Palace, lined with shops, cafés and old buildings. It is lively in the evening, with views over the valley lights.

### Gun Hill

The second-highest point in Mussoorie, reached by a short **ropeway** from the Mall Road or a steep walk. On clear days you can see the peaks of the Garhwal Himalaya.

### Camel's Back Road

A quiet, 3 km walk along the hillside, named after a rock formation shaped like a camel's hump. It's best at sunset.

### Kempty Falls

About 15 km from town, a waterfall that falls into pools where visitors can bathe. It is very crowded in summer and on weekends; go early.

### George Everest's House

The restored house and laboratory of **Sir George Everest**, the Surveyor General of India after whom Mount Everest is named, on a ridge at Park Estate. The views from here are superb, and it is a popular sunrise and sunset spot.

### Company Garden

A landscaped garden with flowers, a small lake and boating, popular with families.

### Jharipani Falls and Bhatta Falls

Smaller waterfalls on the way up from Dehradun, best after the monsoon.

## Landour

Above Mussoorie, **Landour** is a peaceful cantonment with pine and deodar forests, colonial cottages and very little traffic. The author **Ruskin Bond** has long lived here.

- **The Chakkar:** a loop road around the hilltop, perfect for a slow walk.
- **Lal Tibba:** the highest point in the area, with telescopes pointing at the Himalayan peaks.
- **Char Dukan:** a cluster of old shops and cafés famous for pancakes, bun omelettes and hot chocolate.
- **St Paul's Church** and **Kellogg Church**: old churches in beautiful settings.
- **Landour Bakehouse:** known for its baked goods.

## Day trips

- **Dhanaulti:** about 25 km away, with eco parks in deodar forests and snow in winter.
- **Surkanda Devi Temple:** a hilltop temple near Dhanaulti, reached by a short ropeway or trek, with sweeping views.
- **Kanatal:** a quieter village for camping and forest walks.

## The winter line

Between October and December, on clear evenings, you may see the **winter line**, a distinct band of colour on the horizon at sunset caused by an atmospheric effect. Mussoorie is one of the few places where it can be seen.

## Best time to visit

- **March to June:** the most popular season, pleasant and busy.
- **July to September:** monsoon greenery, but landslides and heavy rain.
- **October and November:** clear views and the winter line.
- **December to February:** cold, with occasional snow in Mussoorie and more at Dhanaulti.

## A 2-day plan

1. **Day 1:** Mall Road, Gun Hill ropeway, and sunset on Camel's Back Road.
2. **Day 2:** morning walk in Landour (the Chakkar, Lal Tibba, Char Dukan), George Everest's House in the afternoon, and Kempty Falls if you like.

Add a day for Dhanaulti and Surkanda Devi.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is Mussoorie from Dehradun?

About 35 km, roughly 1 to 1.5 hours by road.

### Is Landour worth visiting?

Yes. It is much quieter than Mussoorie, with lovely walks, views and cafés.

### Does it snow in Mussoorie?

Occasionally, between December and February. Dhanaulti gets more snow.
