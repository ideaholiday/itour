Varkala is one of the most striking beach towns in India. Its long red laterite cliffs drop straight onto the sand, with cafés, yoga studios and guesthouses lined along the top, and the Arabian Sea stretching away below. It is also a pilgrimage town, with an ancient Vishnu temple and a beach where Hindus perform rites for their ancestors. Varkala is easy to reach from Thiruvananthapuram and pairs well with the rest of Kerala.

## How to reach Varkala

- **By air:** Thiruvananthapuram International Airport is about 50 km away, roughly 1.5 hours by road.
- **By train:** **Varkala Sivagiri** station is on the main Kerala line, with trains from Kochi, Thiruvananthapuram and further afield.
- **By road:** Kochi is about 160 km away.

## The cliffs

### North Cliff

The heart of Varkala for travellers: a cliff-top path lined with cafés, restaurants, bookshops, and Ayurveda and yoga centres. It is lovely at sunset, when the whole cliff turns orange.

### South Cliff

Quieter, with more upmarket resorts and views over Papanasam beach.

**Safety:** the cliffs are made of soft laterite and parts of the edge have been eroding. Stay on the paths, keep back from the edge, and take care after dark.

## The beaches

- **Papanasam Beach:** the main beach below the cliffs. Its name means "washing away of sins", and Hindus perform **bali tharpanam** (rites for ancestors) here, especially on Karkidaka Vavu in July or August.
- **Black Beach and Odayam Beach:** to the north, with dark sand and fewer people.
- **Kappil Beach:** about 8 km north, where the backwaters meet the sea.

**Swimming:** the sea at Varkala can have strong currents and rip tides. Swim only between the lifeguard flags, never alone, and never after drinking. Stay out of the water in the monsoon.

## Temples and heritage

- **Janardhana Swamy Temple:** an ancient Vishnu temple on a hill above the beach, believed to be around 2,000 years old. Non-Hindus may not be allowed into the inner sanctum.
- **Sivagiri Mutt:** the ashram and samadhi of the social reformer **Sree Narayana Guru**, a pilgrimage centre that draws huge crowds in late December and early January.
- **Anjengo Fort:** an old English East India Company fort and lighthouse, about 15 km away.

## Backwaters near Varkala

- **Kappil Lake:** kayaking and short boat rides where the backwaters meet the sea.
- **Ponnumthuruthu (Golden Island):** a small island in the backwaters with an old temple, reached by boat.

## Ayurveda and yoga

Varkala is one of Kerala's best-known places for **Ayurveda**. Many centres offer massages and multi-day treatments; for longer treatments, choose a centre with qualified doctors, and ideally one with government classification. Yoga classes and teacher trainings run throughout the season.

## Surfing

Varkala has consistent waves, and several surf schools offer lessons for beginners, especially from October to April.

## What to eat

The cliff-top cafés serve everything from **fresh seafood** (pick your fish and have it grilled) to smoothie bowls. Try Kerala classics like **fish moilee**, **appam with stew**, **puttu and kadala curry**, and a traditional **sadya** on a banana leaf.

## Best time to visit

**October to March** is the best season, with calm seas and sunny days. April and May are hot and humid. The monsoon (June to September) brings heavy rain, rough seas and many closed cafés, but it is also the traditional season for Ayurvedic treatments.

## A 3-day plan

1. **Day 1:** North Cliff walk, Papanasam beach and sunset at the cliff.
2. **Day 2:** Janardhana Swamy Temple in the morning, Ayurveda massage, Black Beach in the afternoon.
3. **Day 3:** Kappil backwaters and Golden Island by boat, or a surf lesson.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Varkala good for swimming?

Only in calm conditions, between the lifeguard flags. Currents can be strong.

### How many days are enough for Varkala?

Two to three days, or longer if you want to relax, surf or take Ayurvedic treatments.

### Varkala or Kovalam: which is better?

Varkala has the dramatic cliffs and a relaxed backpacker feel. Kovalam, closer to Thiruvananthapuram, has more resorts and a lighthouse beach.
